# Hacker News Playbook (Show HN)

> Best for: the *technical* story behind your product. HN upvotes engineering depth and punishes marketing. Only use when you have a genuinely interesting technical angle.

## When to Show HN (Astraea example)

Good angle: "I built a deterministic astrology engine — the LLM never touches the math, zero tokens per reading." That's a real engineering decision with tradeoffs to discuss.

Bad angle: "Show HN: AI fortune-telling app for love 💖🔮" — reads as marketing, gets flagged.

## The post

- **Title:** `Show HN: <thing you built>, <what's technically interesting>` — plain, no emoji, no hype
- **Body (first comment by you):** 3–5 paragraphs: what it is, the hard technical part, what you'd do differently, what's next. Link the repo if open-source (this kit's philosophy helps here)
- **Timing:** submit **Mon–Thu, 8–9am ET**. Avoid US holidays and big news days
- **Be present:** reply to technical questions for the first 6 hours. "Great question" + a real answer. Admit what you don't know

## Do / Don't

✅ Lead with the engineering: architecture, tradeoffs, numbers (latency, cost, accuracy);
✅ open-source something (even a piece) — HN rewards it disproportionately;
❌ ask friends to upvote — vote brigading gets the post killed and your account flagged;
❌ get defensive about criticism — the best Show HNs have the maker saying "fair point, here's why we chose it";
❌ submit more than once — if it flops, wait months and come back with something new

## After

- A good Show HN = 5k–15k views and hundreds of GitHub stars in a day ( compounding if you hit the front page)
- Capture: pin your landing link in your HN profile *before* posting; `?ref=hackernews` on the link you share in comments
- Follow-up: write the "what I learned from Show HN" post — second wave of traffic

## Metrics → `analytics/tracker.csv`

Points, rank, comments, repo stars, site visits, signups. Note which technical detail sparked the longest thread — that's your next blog post.
